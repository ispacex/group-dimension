# `D₅` and the third invariant of `Q_ab` (class ≤ 4): the proof on paper

These results are **not in the paper** (S. Vorobev, *On the inclusion `D₅(G) ⊆ γ₄(G)`*,
https://ispacex.github.io/d5), and the paper is not being changed to include them. This note is
the public account of their proof on paper, from the author's research notes (2026-11-12,
Theorems 12.6 and 11.2 there). They are formalized in this repository as
`GroupDimension.D_five_pow_eq_one_of_third_invariant` and
`GroupDimension.D_five_eq_bot_of_two_large_invariants` (`ThirdInvariant.lean`,
`ThirdInvariantFinite.lean`; blueprint node `own:thm126`) and are compared for Palomar
(`Challenge.lean`) from the version after version 5. They generalize the theorem of
[`ab399.md`](ab399.md) (type `[3, 9, 9]`), which is the case `μ = 1` for one type of `Q_ab`.

**Provenance.** The starting point was a note by the model Kimi K3.1 Pro (Moonshot AI, through
Open Math OS), written at the author's direction, which bounds each term of the identity (⋆) below
separately; it gives the case `μ ≤ 1` and, for `μ ≥ 2`, only a weaker bound. The bound for all
`μ` (the treatment of the top pair through `ω(c̄_{s-1,s}, ·)`, step 3 below) was worked out by a
Claude Code agent. The case `μ ≤ 1` (Theorem 11.2) was also obtained independently in a separate
task by a Claude Code agent, and by Kimi K3.1 Pro by a different route. The argument was then
checked by independent agent reviews, one for each theorem (Theorem 11.2 and Theorem 12.6 each
had a single agent review); the review of Theorem 12.6 found one gap (the choice of `y` for the term
`w'_{s-1,s,s}` when `s ∈ N`, step 3 below) and minor issues, all corrected. **No human expert has
reviewed it.** The term-by-term bound was also tested by computer on 618 random groups of class 4
with cyclic `γ₄` (`C₂₇` or `C₉`; types such as `[9, 27, 27]` and `[27, 27, 27]`), with no violation
(on the weaker lattice of `τ` given by (c1) and (B), (D₂) modulo 3, where the test has content in
100 of the groups); that is evidence, not proof. The Lean proof (`ThirdInvariant.lean`,
`ThirdInvariantFinite.lean`) was written by a Claude Code agent; about half of its lines (helper
lemmas and the finite reduction) are carried over, verbatim or nearly so, from the Kimi-written
`Ab399.lean` and `Ab399Finite.lean`, and it rests on the Kimi-written identity (⋆) in `Star/`; a
Kimi K3.1 Pro run on its skeleton was not used. It is checked by the Lean kernel and uses only
`propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. Novelty is not claimed: no literature search specific to
these statements has been made.

## Statements

Let `Q` be a finite 3-group of class `≤ 4`, write `Q_ab = Q/γ₂(Q) ≅ C_{d(1)} × ⋯ × C_{d(s)}` with
`d(1) ≤ ⋯ ≤ d(s)`, and let `3^μ = d(s-2)` be the third largest invariant (`μ = 0` if `s ≤ 2`).

**Theorem 12.6.** `D₅(Q) ⊆ Ω_{μ-1}(γ₄(Q))`, where `Ω_j(B) = {b ∈ B : b^{3^j} = 1}` and
`Ω_{-1} = 1`. There is no condition on `γ₄(Q)`.

**Theorem 11.2** (the case `μ ≤ 1`). If at most two invariants of `Q_ab` are `≥ 9`, then
`D₅(Q) = 1`, with no condition on `γ₄(Q)`.

**What is formalized.** In Lean, Theorem 12.6 is the exponent bound `z^{3^{μ-1}} = 1` for every
`z ∈ D₅(Q)` (natural subtraction, so `z = 1` for `μ ≤ 1`); the part `D₅(Q) ⊆ γ₄(Q)` of
`Ω_{μ-1}(γ₄(Q))` is not formalized with it. The hypothesis on `μ` is stated intrinsically, for any
`μ` with at most two invariants larger than `3^μ`: the subgroup `Q_ab^{3^μ}` of `3^μ`-th powers has
at most `9` elements of order dividing `3` (for `Q_ab ≅ ∏ C_{3^{k_i}}` it has `3^{#{i : k_i > μ}}`).
`Q` a 3-group is the hypothesis `IsPGroup 3 Q`, class `≤ 4` is
`(⊤ : Subgroup Q).lowerCentralSeries 4 = ⊥`. Only these finite forms are formalized; the general
form of the notes (`D₅(G) = γ₅(G)` whenever `G_ab` is a finite 3-group with at most two invariants
`> 3`, through `G/γ₅(G)`) is not.

## Setting (from the paper, §6, proof of Theorem 6.11)

Notation as in the paper and in [`ab399.md`](ab399.md): Tahara's uniqueness bases `x_i` with
`d(i) = 3^{α_i}` the order of `x_i` modulo `γ₂`, the commutators `c_{ij}` and their classes `c̄_{ij}`
in `L₂ = γ₂/γ₃`, `Y_i = x_i^{d(i)} ∈ γ₂` with class `Ȳ_i`, the bilinear map `ω : L₂ × L₂ → γ₄`,
`ω(ā, b̄) = [a, b]`, the ternary form `t(ā; x̄, ȳ) = [a, x, y]`, and Tahara's generator `g(τ)` of
`D₅` for an integer family `τ = (u, q, w', w'', …)` satisfying his conditions (A)–(F) (Tahara 1981,
Theorem 6.1, the inclusion part, formalized as `taharaFiveOne`). The basis is adapted as in step (4)
of the proof of Theorem 6.11: the index set is `A ⊔ N` with `Ȳ_h ∈ 3L₂` for `h ∈ N` and the classes
`Ȳ_h`, `h ∈ A`, independent modulo `3L₂`, and the congruences (c1)–(c3) hold:
(c1) `3 ∣ u_{ih}` when `d(i) = d(h)`; (c2) `3 ∣ u_{ih}` when `h ∈ A`; (c3) `3 ∣ w'_{igh}` when
`g ∈ A`, and `3 ∣ w''_{jkh}` when `h ∈ A`. Also `3 ∣ q_{ih}` when `d(i) < d(h)`.

**The exact identity (⋆).** Steps (1)–(3) of the proof of Theorem 6.11 do not use the exponent of
`γ₄` (paper, Remark 6.13) and give, for every admissible `τ`,
`2 g(τ) = T₁ + ⋯ + T₅` (written additively in `γ₃`; Lean: `Star.two_smul_gen_eq`). As in
`ab399.md`, `T₅ = 0`. Two lemmas of the notes (§10.2 there) bring the rest into closed form.
(i) The `t`-part of `T₁` vanishes: for `i < h` its coefficient is `u_{ih} C_{d(h)}` with
`v₃(C_{3^β}) = β − 1`, the bracket is killed by `d(i)` (as `d(i) c̄_{ih} = 0`), and when
`d(i) = d(h)` (c1) gives `3 ∣ u_{ih}`; what is left of `T₁` is
`Σ_{i<h} u_{ih} (d(h)-1)/2 · (q_{ih} ω(c̄_{ih}, Ȳ_i) − ω(c̄_{ih}, Ȳ_h))`.
(ii) For `h < i`, `q_{hi} [Y_h, x_i, x_i] = ω(c̄_{hi}, Ȳ_i)` (by (K1)–(K4) of the paper), so
`T₂ = −Σ_{h<i} u_{hi} ω(c̄_{hi}, Ȳ_i)`. Substituting (i), (ii) and `T₅ = 0` into (⋆) and renaming
the indices of `T₂` gives, for `τ` satisfying (B), (C₁), (D₂), (E), (F₂), the *remainder*

    R(τ) = Σ_{i<h} u_{ih} ( (d(h)-1)/2 · q_{ih} ω(c̄_{ih}, Ȳ_i) − (d(h)+1)/2 · ω(c̄_{ih}, Ȳ_h) )
           − Σ_{i<g≤h} w'_{igh} [Y_g, x_i, x_h] − Σ_{j<k<h} w''_{jkh} ω(Ȳ_h, c̄_{jk}),

with `2 g(τ) = R(τ)`. A *term* is one summand: the `q`-term and the `h`-term of a pair `(i, h)`,
the `w'`-term of `(i, g, h)`, the `w''`-term of `(j, k, h)`. Its *distinguished index* is `i` in
the first two sums and `j` in the third. (In Lean the coefficients of `T₁` come from the general
degree-`n` defect `ThirdInvariant.Setup.delta_general`, with `n = d(h)`, `(n-1)/2` and
`K_n = C(n,3) − (n-1)/2 · C(n,2) = −n(n²−1)/12`; `3^m ∣ K_{3^{m+1}}` is `K_dvd`.)

## The proof

**Lemma 12.5.** (a) For `i < h` the forms `ω(c̄_{ih}, ·)` and `t(·; x̄_i, ·)` are killed by `d(i)`.
(b) `ω(c̄_{s-1,s}, L₂) ⊆ Ω_μ(γ₄)`. (c) `[Y_s, x_{s-1}, x_s] = −ω(c̄_{s-1,s}, Ȳ_s)`.

*Proof.* (a) `x_i^{d(i)} ∈ γ₂`, so `d(i) c̄_{ih} = 0`, and the forms are additive (class `≤ 4`).
(b) `L₂` is spanned by the `c̄_{ab}`, `a < b`; `ω(c̄_{s-1,s}, c̄_{s-1,s}) = 0` (the form is
alternating), and every other pair has `a ≤ s − 2`, so `3^μ c̄_{ab} = d(s-2) c̄_{ab} = 0`.
(c) By (K5) of the paper (the Hall–Witt identity in class `≤ 4`), `[Y_s, x_{s-1}, x_s] − [Y_s, x_s, x_{s-1}] =
ω(Ȳ_s, c̄_{s-1,s})`, and `[Y_s, x_s] = 1`. ∎ (Lean: `omega_top`, `omega_le`, `cY`, `cYlow`.)

**Proof of Theorem 12.6.**

1. **Every term is `3y`.** The factor 3 is found as in the proof of Theorem 6.11:
   * `q`-term of `(i, h)`: `3 ∣ q_{ih}` if `d(i) < d(h)`, `3 ∣ u_{ih}` if `d(i) = d(h)` (c1);
   * `h`-term: `3 ∣ u_{ih}` if `h ∈ A` (c2); if `h ∈ N`, `Ȳ_h = 3ā` and
     `ω(c̄_{ih}, Ȳ_h) = 3 ω(c̄_{ih}, ā)`;
   * `w'`-term: `3 ∣ w'_{igh}` if `g ∈ A` (c3); if `g ∈ N`, `[Y_g, x_i, x_h] = 3 t(ā; x̄_i, x̄_h)`;
   * `w''`-term: `3 ∣ w''_{jkh}` if `h ∈ A` (c3); if `h ∈ N`, `ω(3ā, c̄_{jk}) = 3 ω(ā, c̄_{jk})`.

   It remains to show `y ∈ Ω_μ(γ₄)`, i.e. `3^μ y = 0`.
2. **Distinguished index `≤ s − 2`.** All forms in the term are killed by
   `d(distinguished) ∣ d(s-2) = 3^μ` (Lemma 12.5 (a); for `w''`, `d(j) c̄_{jk} = 0`).
3. **Distinguished index `s − 1`** (the top pair). Then `h = s` in the first sums,
   `(i, g, h) = (s-1, s, s)` in the second, and the third is empty. Every value is
   `ω(c̄_{s-1,s}, ·)`: directly for the `u`-terms, and by Lemma 12.5 (c) for `w'_{s-1,s,s}`. When
   `s ∈ N`, take `y = w'_{s-1,s,s} ω(c̄_{s-1,s}, ā)` (the term is
   `−w'_{s-1,s,s} · (−3 ω(c̄_{s-1,s}, ā))`), **not** `w' t(ā; x̄_{s-1}, x̄_s)` from step 1: the latter
   need not lie in `Ω_μ`, since `t(ā; x̄_{s-1}, x̄_s) = t(ā; x̄_s, x̄_{s-1}) − ω(c̄_{s-1,s}, ā)` and
   `[a, x_s] ≠ 1` in general. By Lemma 12.5 (b), every such `y` lies in `Ω_μ(γ₄)`.
4. So `2 g(τ) = R(τ) ∈ 3 Ω_μ(γ₄) ⊆ Ω_{μ-1}(γ₄)`. Since `γ₃` is an abelian group of odd order,
   `g(τ) ∈ Ω_{μ-1}(γ₄)` for every admissible `τ` (Lean: `ThirdInvariant.Setup.gen_pow_eq_one`,
   `g(τ)^{3^{μ-1}} = 1`, proved by applying `3^{μ-1}` to (⋆) term by term, `T1_eq` … `T5_eq`).
   By the inclusion part of Tahara's Theorem 6.1, `D₅(Q)` lies in the subgroup generated by the
   `g(τ)`, so `D₅(Q) ⊆ Ω_{μ-1}(γ₄(Q))`. For `μ ≤ 1`, `3 Ω_μ = 0`, which is Theorem 11.2. ∎

**The finite reduction** (`ThirdInvariantFinite.lean`) builds Tahara's data and the adapted basis
from `Q` as `Ab399Finite.lean` does, and derives from the socle hypothesis that `d(i) ∣ 3^μ`
whenever two indices lie above `i`: three indices `i < j < k` with `d(i) ∤ 3^μ` would give `27`
elements `Σ a_l (d(l)/3) x̄_l` (`l ∈ {i, j, k}`, `a_l ∈ {0, 1, 2}`) of order dividing 3 among the
`3^μ`-th powers of `Q_ab`.

**Limits.** The estimate uses of Tahara's conditions only (C₁), (B) and (D₂) modulo 3 (through
(c1)–(c3)); on that weaker lattice of `τ` the bound `Ω_{μ-1}` is attained in computer examples
(types `[9, 27, 27]`, `[27, 27, 27]`), so `D₅ = 1` for three invariants `≥ 9` (`μ ≥ 2`) would need
more of Tahara's conditions. That case is open in general (with no condition on `γ₄`); the notes'
computer-assisted results for it under `γ₄⁹ = 1` are not formalized and not claimed here.
